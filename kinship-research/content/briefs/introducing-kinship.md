title: "Introducing Kinship: following the reproduction of advantage"
date: 2026-11-06
summary: "A research agenda for understanding where economic advantage originates and why it persists."
---

Our starting point is simple: **inequality is not just a snapshot of who earns what today.** It is also a dynamic process in which resources, risks, and opportunities accumulate and pass across generations.

Families sit at the center of that process. Parents invest in children, but they also transfer money to adults, connect relatives to opportunities, share risks, and make choices about whom to support. Partnerships form and dissolve. Siblings may receive different resources. Grandparents matter, too.

Yet we do not want to study families in isolation. Education systems, neighborhoods, labor markets, housing, taxation and social insurance can reproduce inherited advantages — or weaken them.

### Three questions we will keep returning to

1. **Origins:** Which events and institutions create durable economic differences?
2. **Persistence:** Why do some advantages survive across decades and generations?
3. **Mechanisms:** What do families, networks, and institutions actually do to transmit them?

We will curate work from major economics journals, working-paper series, and relevant neighboring fields. Papers will be judged primarily by what they contribute to these questions, not simply by the name of the outlet.

This first version is a **design and content prototype**. It is not yet a live weekly research digest.
