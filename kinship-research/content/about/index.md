## The question

**How are economic advantages and disadvantages created, sustained, and transmitted across generations?**

Kinship is an independent research-discovery project centered on the origins and persistence of economic inequality. Families and kinship networks are central to this question: they transmit resources, influence opportunities, provide insurance, and shape life trajectories long after childhood ends.

But inequality is not created by families alone. Schools, neighborhoods, labor markets, social networks and public institutions are equally important parts of the story.

## What we cover

Our editorial lens connects intergenerational mobility, wealth and inheritance, family structure, parental investment, human capital, household finance, and public policy. We also follow adjacent research when it helps explain the persistence of advantage.

## How we select research

We prioritize substantive relevance and empirical credibility. Publication in a leading journal is one useful signal, but an excellent early working paper from a specialized institution may be more useful than an unrelated article in a top-five journal.

The archive tracks a **paper as one intellectual work**, even when it has multiple working-paper versions or later appears in a journal. Future importers will need DOI/identifier matching and manual review to avoid duplicates.

## Current status

This is an **early prototype**: it contains a small set of verified, historically influential papers and a candidate source registry. It is not yet a comprehensive archive, live newsletter, or automated recommendation engine. All publication dates reflect the studies, not a claim that the articles are newly released.

## Editorial transparency

Descriptions are brief editorial summaries and should be checked against the original articles. No citation counts, causal claims or editorial ratings should be presented without a documented basis. The source registry and paper metadata are intended to be version-controlled and auditable.
